Case presentation
A 16-year-old right-hand-dominant male with no significant past medical or family history and no prior trauma or radiation exposure presented with a large swelling over his right scapular region. The mass had been gradually increasing in size over several months. Initially associated with mild discomfort, the pain became progressively more severe, eventually leading to functional limitation of his right shoulder movement. His body mass index was 22.4 kg/m2, and his Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status was 1.

On physical examination, a massive, dome-shaped mass was observed on the patient’s right upper back (Fig. 1). The overlying skin was stretched, with visible striae, and the mass itself was firm to hard on palpation, with deep fixation, suggesting origin from the underlying bone. The mass measured approximately 18 cm in greatest diameter. There was no palpable lymphadenopathy. Range of motion of the right shoulder was significantly limited, with active forward flexion to 90°, abduction to 70°, and external rotation to 30°, limited by pain and mechanical obstruction. The neurovascular examination of the right upper extremity was normal, with intact motor function and sensation. Laboratory studies revealed an elevated serum alkaline phosphatase (ALP) level of 425 U/L (normal, 40–150 U/L) and a lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) level of 320 U/L (normal, 100–200 U/L). Complete blood count and comprehensive metabolic panel were within normal limits.

MRI of the right shoulder revealed a large, heterogeneous mass arising from the right scapula (Fig. 2). The lesion measured 17.5 × 12.0 × 9.5 cm and demonstrated mixed signal intensity on both T1-weighted (intermediate to low signal) and T2-weighted (heterogeneously hyperintense) sequences, consistent with necrosis, hemorrhage, and tumor matrix. The mass showed extensive soft tissue extension, encasing the infraspinatus and subscapularis muscles, though without clear invasion of the thoracic cavity (Fig. 2B, axial T2-weighted image). Post-contrast sequences demonstrated heterogeneous enhancement, with areas of necrosis. The lesion did not involve the glenohumeral joint. Figure 2.Magnetic resonance imaging of the right scapula. Staging investigations included a contrast-enhanced CT of the chest, which demonstrated no pulmonary metastases. A whole-body technetium-99 m methylene diphosphonate bone scan revealed intense radiotracer uptake localized to the right scapular region, with no evidence of distant osseous metastases.

A subsequent core needle biopsy of the mass yielded histopathology showing a highly cellular malignant mesenchymal tumor composed of pleomorphic spindle and polygonal cells. The cells contained hyperchromatic nuclei with high mitotic activity (15 mitoses per 10 high-power fields), and malignant osteoid deposition was present, leading to a diagnosis of high-grade osteoblastic osteosarcoma. Immunohistochemistry was performed and showed diffuse positivity for SATB2 and osteocalcin, with negative staining for cytokeratin, EMA, and S-100, excluding alternative diagnoses.

The patient received neoadjuvant chemotherapy following the Methotrexate, Adriamycin (doxorubicin), and Platinol (cisplatin) (MAP) protocol, which included high-dose methotrexate (10 g/m2 IV) with leucovorin rescue, doxorubicin (75 mg/m2 IV), and cisplatin (120 mg/m2 IV). Three preoperative cycles were administered at 3-week intervals. The patient tolerated chemotherapy reasonably well; no dose reductions were required.

Imaging following neoadjuvant therapy showed only a mild reduction in tumor vascularity, with no significant decrease in tumor size.

Following neoadjuvant chemotherapy, the patient underwent a radical en bloc resection with total scapulectomy. The surgical procedure was performed with the patient in the lateral decubitus position. An elliptical incision was made over the tumor, encompassing the previous biopsy tract. Wide soft tissue dissection was performed to achieve adequate margins, with a planned 2-cm margin of normal tissue. The axillary vessels and brachial plexus were identified and preserved (Fig. 3). The tumor was found to be massive, replacing the entire scapula with extensive involvement of the surrounding muscles. No gross invasion of the chest wall or the neurovascular bundle was identified. After detaching all muscular attachments, the entire scapula with the tumor was removed en bloc. No bony reconstruction was performed, with a flail shoulder accepted to avoid implant-related complications, in accordance with the patient’s and family’s preferences after a thorough discussion of the risks and benefits.

Gross examination of the postoperative specimen revealed a large, lobulated mass replacing the scapula, measuring 18.5 × 13.0 × 10.0 cm, with a variegated cut surface showing gray-white tumor areas, hemorrhage, and necrosis. The tumor extended into the surrounding soft tissues, with a maximum soft-tissue extension of 4.5 cm.

Microscopic examination confirmed a high-grade malignant spindle cell tumor with marked cellular pleomorphism, frequent atypical mitoses, abundant malignant osteoid formation, and areas of chondroid differentiation.

The tumor showed no vascular invasion, no perineural invasion, and no lymphovascular invasion. The pathological stage was pT2bN0M0 (AJCC stage IIB). The final diagnosis was high-grade osteoblastic osteosarcoma with a good histological response to chemotherapy (Huvos grade IV, greater than 90% necrosis).

The patient’s postoperative recovery was uneventful. The surgical wound healed primarily, and the patient was discharged on postoperative day 5. He was fitted with a shoulder sling for comfort and instructed on passive range-of-motion exercises for the elbow and hand. No adjuvant radiotherapy was administered.

The patient completed adjuvant chemotherapy, receiving three additional cycles of the MAP protocol (three cycles preoperatively and three cycles postoperatively, for a total of six cycles). The patient tolerated adjuvant chemotherapy well, with no dose delays or significant complications.

At 18 months, the patient remains disease-free on surveillance MRI (6, 12, and 18 mo) and chest CT (3, 6, 12, and 18 mo), with normalized ALP (85 U/L) and LDH (145 U/L). Functional outcomes: Musculoskeletal Tumor Society (MSTS) 63% (19/30), Toronto Extremity Salvage Score (TESS) 68%; active shoulder forward flexion 45°, abduction 30°, external rotation 15°, internal rotation to L5; normal hand and elbow function; VAS pain score 2/10 (mild, intermittent). He is independent in daily activities (using compensatory strategies for overhead tasks), wears a shoulder orthosis for prolonged activities, and reports satisfaction with return to studies and social life.
