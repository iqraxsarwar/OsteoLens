Case Presentation
A 23-year-old woman with no significant past medical or family history presented to the ENT department in February 2025 with a 7-month history of persistent pain in the right earlobe and external auditory canal, initially noted in July 2024. Over the preceding weeks, she developed swelling in the external auditory canal, prompting further evaluation. On physical examination, a lesion was identified in the canal and subsequently biopsied, with initial pathology reporting a cholesteatoma, after which the patient underwent tumor resection via a middle cranial fossa approach with right canaloplasty.

However, final histopathology revealed an osteosarcoma of the middle cranial fossa with unknown surgical margins. The patient was referred to an oncology center, where imaging less than 1 month postoperatively demonstrated locoregional recurrence with clinical and imagenologic unresectable criteria. During routine laboratory workup, a positive serum pregnancy test was discovered. Obstetric ultrasound was performed, showing a fetal biometry of 24.3 weeks with an estimated weight of 602 ± 101 g (9th percentile, Hadlock), normal amniotic fluid, and a fundal placenta.

Given the aggressive nature of the tumor, initial recommendations included pregnancy termination followed by systemic palliative chemotherapy with doxorubicin and cisplatin. After multidisciplinary counseling, the patient decided to continue the pregnancy; therefore, palliative chemoradiation for local tumor control was given, consisting of ifosfamide/mesna combined with 40 Gy of radiotherapy ( Fig. 1 ). Afterwards, the patient reported decreased fetal movements; during ultrasonographic evaluation, anhydramnios was reported, the reason for which she was referred to a tertiary hospital for further obstetric and maternal fetal evaluation, where an ultrasound evaluation confirmed anhydramnios. Crystallization and PAMG-1 determination tests were performed, ruling out premature membrane rupture as cause of anhydramnios. The patient was admitted for close maternal and fetal monitoring, with serial complete blood count (CBC), C-reactive protein (CRP), and erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR; Fig. 2 ) and twice daily non-stress test (NST). Serial laboratory tests showed no signs of systemic infection. Assessment by the genetics department concluded that the fetal diagnoses of early-onset fetal growth restriction and anhydramnios were most likely due to the history of initiation of chemoradiotherapy with ifosfamide/mesna at 24.3 weeks of gestation, consisting of 15 cycles with a total dose of 40 Gy in 15 fractions, stating that pharmacovigilance studies have shown that exposure to ifosfamide between 16 and 30 weeks of gestation is associated with oligohydramnios, fetal growth restriction, and neonatal acute renal failure secondary to fetal nephrotoxicity. 16 As the pregnancy was considered viable and had both fetal growth restriction and anhydramnios, a multidisciplinary meeting was held among the obstetrics, oncology, and neonatology departments. It was decided to proceed with an elective abdominal delivery. Antenatal corticosteroids were administered, as well as fetal neuroprotection with magnesium sulfate for a total of 9 h prior to resolution. A cesarean section was performed, resulting in the birth of a live newborn weighing 970 g and measuring 35.5 cm in length, with a Ballard score consistent with 29 weeks of gestation, Apgar scores of 7 and 9, and a Silverman–Anderson score not assessable. The neonate was subsequently transferred to the neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) for further management and monitoring. Maternal postoperative recovery was adequate, and the patient was discharged and referred to an oncology center with a brain MRI ( Figs. 3 and 4 ) to continue management. The MRI showed right temporal mass extending through the external auditory canal into the intracranial compartment, with infiltration of the temporal lobe and associated vasogenic edema, as well as areas of cystic degeneration and/or central necrosis, consistent with the referring diagnosis.

The neonate remained hospitalized for 58 days, during which time abnormalities associated with maternal chemotherapy exposure were ruled out. He developed respiratory distress syndrome requiring one dose of surfactant and mechanical ventilation for 2 days, followed by nasal cannula oxygen therapy for 46 days. Vasoactive amines were not required, and cardiac function was adequate at discharge. He received pasteurized human milk and was managed with prokinetic agents. He developed non-hemolytic hyperbilirubinemia, for which he underwent 72 h of phototherapy. Serial laboratory testing showed no evidence of acute kidney injury or liver dysfunction. Renal function remained stable throughout hospitalization, as demonstrated by serial serum creatinine measurements, with the highest value recorded on day 6 of life (Cr: 0.59 mg/dL). The estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), calculated using the Schwartz formula, was 19.85 mL/min/1.73 m 2 , which is within the expected range for a preterm neonate of this age. During hospitalization, he presented anemia associated with prematurity, requiring three packed red blood cell transfusions, without evidence of additional cytopenias or toxicity. No clinical signs of neurological injury were observed, and multiple cranial ultrasound examinations showed no evidence of hemorrhage.
