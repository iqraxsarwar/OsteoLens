2. Case Report
A woman in her 60s was transferred from an outside hospital for an endobronchial mass associated with rapidly progressive airway compromise. She had been treated empirically for pneumonia for several months before eventually undergoing computed tomography (CT) of the chest, which revealed a large right lung mass (Figure 1). On arrival, she required 2 L nasal cannula oxygen to maintain a saturation of 92%. A PET scan performed a few weeks prior showed a right lower lobe moss‐like density extending into the right airway centrally with a max SUV of 11.6 with no evidence of regional nodal or distant metastatic disease.

Interventional pulmonology performed urgent flexible bronchoscopy for tumor debulking, with rigid bronchoscopy and ECMO on standby given the concern for potential airway loss. Bronchoscopy revealed the tumor completely obstructing the right mainstem bronchus and protruding across the main carina causing partial obstruction of the distal trachea and left mainstem bronchus. The left mainstem had a tumor protruding across it, narrowing it by ~80% endobronchially. Debulking was performed using snare resection and forceps. Jet ventilation was utilized during snare excision due to the risk that dislodged tumor fragments could occlude the left mainstem bronchus. The tumor was debrided to the level of the proximal right mainstem bronchus to preserve the patency of the left mainstem bronchus. Due to the pedunculated nature of the mass, the decision to use a hot snare was made, hoping this would allow for resection of large amounts of the mass while ensuring airway patency. Although the lesion appeared more like soft tissue during endobronchial evaluation, significant difficulty was encountered during hot snare resection as the snare was unable to transect the tumor despite optimization of electrocautery settings. This was suspected to be secondary to calcific deposition related to the patient’s known osteosarcoma, resulting in minimal soft tissue for effective electrocautery. The decision was made to switch to mechanical debulking with the snare, which was ultimately successful, although substantial resistance was encountered.

The procedure was performed using flexible bronchoscopy alone. Rigid bronchoscopy was not utilized as there was minimal bleeding, and it was not felt to provide additional procedural benefit. In addition, there was no clear plane to core out the tumor because the lesion did not appear adherent to the right mainstem bronchial wall and instead appeared to emanate from the right lower lobe.

Overall, the bronchoscopic intervention successfully achieved the primary goal of maintaining ventilation to the left lung, while the right lung remained extensively involved by the tumor.

Histopathology demonstrated a high‐grade spindle cell neoplasm with malignant osteoid, consistent with metastatic osteosarcoma (Figure 2). The patient reported a history of localized distal left femur osteosarcoma treated with chemotherapy and surgical resection in 2011. Given her remote history of distal femoral osteosarcoma, the lesion was interpreted as metastatic osteosarcoma. Original pathology records from the 2011 tumor were unavailable for comparison. She reported that after her initial diagnosis of distal left femur osteosarcoma, she had undergone annual CT surveillance for 5 years. Then, 4 months prior to presenting at our facility, she developed respiratory symptoms, for which her primary care physician ordered chest radiographs. These radiographs suggested she had pneumonia, for which she received antibiotics; however, her symptoms continued, prompting a chest CT that showed an endobronchial lesion.

Surgical resection was deemed the only viable option to prevent fatal airway obstruction. Preoperative PET imaging suggested the patency of the right upper lobe bronchus, and a right middle and lower bilobectomy or pneumonectomy was planned. Intraoperatively, however, tumor extension across the fissures precluded the preservation of the right upper lobe, necessitating a right pneumonectomy. A serratus‐sparing posterolateral thoracotomy was performed.

Dissection was difficult due to the extensive tumor burden limiting lung mobilization. The pulmonary veins were individually dissected and divided, followed by the stapled division of the main pulmonary artery. The right mainstem bronchus was then opened sharply, and the intraluminal tumor—extending proximally but without evidence of invasion—was removed. The right mainstem bronchus was stapled at the level of the carina. A pedicled intercostal muscle flap was harvested and used to buttress the bronchial stump.

The patient tolerated the procedure well. Postoperative pathology revealed a 14.5 cm metastatic osteosarcoma originating in the right middle lobe (Figure 3). Lymph nodes and all margins, including bronchial and vascular, were all negative. Preoperative imaging and bronchoscopic evaluation suggested that the tumor arose from the right lower lobe, with extensive extension into the bronchus intermedius and right mainstem bronchus. However, following pneumonectomy, pathologic examination demonstrated that the primary pulmonary tumor originated in the right middle lobe. The discrepancy was likely related to the extensive tumor burden and transfissural extension, with the bulky right middle lobe tumor extending inferiorly toward the right lower lobe and centrally into the bronchus intermedius.

The patient began ifosfamide and etoposide, which were discontinued due to neurotoxicity. The patient established care with medical oncology to continue surveillance imaging every 3 months. Nine months after surgical resection, the patient was noted to have multiple pulmonary nodules on CT thorax, concerning metastatic disease. The patient initially declined therapy with regorafenib, a multikinase inhibitor, and opted to continue surveillance. Another 3 months passed, and she was found to have peritoneal metastasis, at which point she agreed to start regorafenib. Unfortunately, the patient continued to have evidence of metastatic growth, and the decision was made to discontinue regorafenib, and the patient is discussing the risks and benefits of starting systemic chemotherapy with either gemcitabine and docetaxel or gemcitabine monotherapy.
