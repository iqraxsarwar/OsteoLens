Case presentation
A 37-year-old woman was referred for the management of a progressively enlarging lesion of the right mandible. Her medical history was notable for asthma. She reported smoking approximately one pack of cigarettes per day, occasional alcohol consumption, and regular evening cannabis use. No other significant medical history was reported.

The patient’s initial symptoms consisted of pain localized to the right posterior mandibular dental region, particularly around tooth 46. The lesion was initially managed as a dental condition, and endodontic treatment of tooth 46 was performed by her general dental practitioner. Despite treatment, the symptoms persisted. Over several months, the patient experienced recurrent episodes of acute right mandibular pain associated with infectious exacerbations and recurrent episodes of right buccal cellulitis. The pain was episodic and resolved between acute episodes, with no persistent pain reported outside these infectious episodes. She presented repeatedly to emergency departments, where some episodes were associated with collections requiring drainage and antibiotic treatment with amoxicillin. Symptoms improved with antibiotic therapy and WHO step II analgesics. This intermittent clinical course and the relief of symptoms following treatment may have contributed to the patient’s limited adherence to follow-up and delayed surgical management. Despite the transient improvement of acute symptoms, the mandibular lesion continued to enlarge over time. The patient eventually developed visible extraoral facial asymmetry due to enlargement of the right mandible, a feature that had not been present initially. Clinical examination revealed no cervical lymphadenopathy. No dental mobility was observed, including at tooth 46 despite the previous endodontic treatment. No sensory or motor neurological deficit was reported.

The initial radiological assessment demonstrated a right intramandibular radiolucent lesion with homogeneous content, irregular borders, and marked cortical expansion. The lesion measured approximately 40 mm in its greatest dimension. The available panoramic radiograph (Figure 1) and cross-sectional imaging (Figure 2) demonstrated involvement of the right posterior mandible, including the horizontal branch and mandibular angle. The lesion appeared expansile and destructive, with marked alteration of the surrounding cortical bone. Because of repeated loss to follow-up and limited compliance, no preoperative biopsy was performed before the initial surgical procedure.

Surgical exploration and excision of the lesion were performed. Intraoperatively, the lesion did not demonstrate the characteristics expected for a conventional odontogenic cyst. Instead, a solid tissue-like proliferation was identified. The intraoperative appearance therefore made a simple dentigerous or inflammatory cyst unlikely (Figures 3, 4). The lesion was submitted for histopathological examination. The intramandibular tumor specimen consisted of a fragment measuring 3.5 × 2.5 × 0.8 cm. A second soft-tissue specimen from the basilar region measured 2.5 × 1 × 1.2 cm. A third specimen of right mandibular bone measured 3 × 0.6 × 0.2 cm. The basilar soft-tissue specimen consisted of a poorly cellular fibrous wall without atypia.

The intramandibular tumor and mandibular bone specimens demonstrated comparable findings. Histological examination revealed a neoplastic proliferation with cartilaginous differentiation organized into nodules. The chondrocytes showed moderate cytological atypia. Numerous binucleated cells were observed, together with enlarged hyperchromatic nuclei and irregular nuclear contours. Part of the lesion was necrotic. No obvious osteoid formation was identified. Importantly, the lesion infiltrated between preexisting bone trabeculae. A small peripheral cystic lesion lined by epithelial cells with strongly eosinophilic cytoplasm was also observed. Its relationship to the main tumor remained uncertain. Immunohistochemical analysis was performed on decalcified material. SATB2 highlighted osteocytes and osteoblasts within infiltrated bone trabeculae. S100 protein staining was negative. KL1 highlighted epithelial cells lining the cystic lesion. The Ki-67 proliferation index was low, within the limitations associated with decalcified material. The initial pathological conclusion was an infiltrative tumor with cartilaginous differentiation suspicious for chondrosarcoma. The pathological material was referred for expert review at the Institut Bergonié, Bordeaux, France, a specialized sarcoma center. Following expert review, the final diagnosis retained was mandibular chondrosarcoma.

The patient underwent complementary staging investigations. Cervicofacial CT demonstrated postoperative changes related to mandibular surgery. A cluster of right submandibular lymph nodes with preserved fatty hila was observed. No other cervical lymphadenopathy was identified. PET/CT demonstrated mild hypermetabolism of the expansile lytic lesion involving the right mandibular angle and horizontal branch, corresponding to the primary lesion. No distant hypermetabolic lesions were identified. The final staging was T4N0M0.

Following multidisciplinary discussion, definitive surgical management was undertaken. The patient underwent right interruptive mandibulectomy with tracheostomy and immediate reconstruction using a vascularized free fibula flap. The mandibulectomy specimen was received fresh and oriented according to its superior, inferior, anterior, and posterior aspects. It measured 7 × 6 × 2.5 cm and included a cutaneous flap measuring 4.5 × 1 cm.

Microscopic examination demonstrated a poorly circumscribed osseous and desmoplastic lesion composed of well-formed bone trabeculae separated by spindle cells arranged in a fascicular pattern. The spindle cells displayed elongated nuclei without significant cytological atypia. The bone trabeculae were arranged in parallel and were focally curved and anastomosing. Despite the relatively bland cytological appearance, the lesion was locally aggressive and infiltrated the mandible, with extension into peripheral striated muscle and fibro-adipose tissue. No tumor emboli were identified. No perineural invasion was observed. Review of the initial biopsy confirmed cartilaginous differentiation. The morphological appearance of the definitive specimen raised the differential diagnosis of low-grade parosteal osteosarcoma.

Immunohistochemical examination demonstrated that the spindle tumor cells were positive for actin and negative for desmin, S100 protein, SOX10, STAT6, CD34, and cytokeratin AE1/AE3. Beta-catenin overexpression was not observed. The Ki-67 proliferation index was approximately 2%. MDM2 and CDK4 testing was not available. Given the discordance between the initial and definitive specimens, expert sarcoma pathology review was crucial. The final diagnosis retained following expert review was mandibular chondrosarcoma. Three right level IIb lymph nodes were examined and demonstrated reactive hyperplasia without evidence of malignancy. The right submandibular gland showed no evidence of malignancy. The anterior and posterior osseous margins were free of tumor. The internal lateral margin measured 0.2 mm, while the external lateral margin measured 2.5 mm.

Following definitive surgery, the patient was admitted to the intensive care unit for postoperative monitoring. No surgical complications occurred. Serial Doppler monitoring of the vascularized free fibula flap demonstrated satisfactory perfusion without evidence of flap compromise. The patient required transfusion of two units of packed red blood cells because of microcytic anemia identified during the preoperative assessment. Multimodal analgesia provided effective postoperative pain control. The postoperative course was also marked by transient rhabdomyolysis, with a peak creatine phosphokinase concentration of 1,300 IU/L. The laboratory abnormalities progressively improved, and no clinical or biological evidence of compartment syndrome was observed. Ventilatory support was rapidly discontinued. The tracheostomy cuff was deflated before discharge from the intensive care unit. The patient was transferred to the otorhinolaryngology department for further postoperative management and was subsequently discharged home.
