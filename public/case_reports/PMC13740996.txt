Case Report
A 12-year-old girl with a known history of a UBC in the left proximal humerus presented with worsening shoulder pain. Eighteen months earlier, she had undergone open biopsy, curettage, and calcium phosphate bone grafting at an outside facility for a benign-appearing lesion (Fig. 1). The initial radiographs demonstrated a radiolucent diaphyseal lesion with cortical thinning and a pathologic fracture (Fig. 1a and b). Although certain features are atypical for a classic UBC, including diaphyseal location, asymmetry, indistinct margins, and periosteal reaction, these are attributable to the acute pathologic fracture rather than an inherently aggressive process. Specifically, the apparent gravity-defying position of the fallen cortical fragment on the anteroposterior view reflects displacement into the surrounding fracture hematoma, a recognized phenomenon in acutely fractured UBCs and not a feature of malignancy. The periosteal reaction visible on the post-treatment radiographs (Fig. 1c and d) reflects the expected healing response following curetting and bone grafting. Histopathology at that time confirmed a UBC, with the outside surgical pathology report documenting scant lymphohistiocytic cells and eosinophils in a hemorrhagic background with no cytologic atypia, mitotic activity, or osteoid production. Although the original histologic slides are unavailable for independent review, as the procedure was performed at an outside institution, this report provides adequate documentation of a benign bone cyst diagnosis. Her post-operative recovery was uneventful. She was symptom-free for 12 months, and then developed progressive shoulder pain without antecedent trauma, constitutional symptoms, or weight loss, leading to re-evaluation 6 months later. There were no other palpable masses or musculoskeletal findings on physical examination. Given her history and new symptoms, repeat imaging was obtained. Radiographs demonstrated a new, aggressive-appearing lytic lesion involving the proximal left humerus, slightly proximal but in continuity with the site of the previously treated UBC (Fig. 2). A staging chest computed tomography (CT) showed no evidence of metastatic disease. Magnetic resonance imaging of the entire humerus revealed a heterogeneous, expansile lesion centered in the proximal humeral metaphysis, with internal fluid-fluid levels and demonstrated heterogeneous post-contrast enhancement (Fig. 3a-e). The patient subsequently underwent a CT-guided biopsy, which revealed a high-grade tumor with focal osteoid and hemorrhagic and cystic features interpreted as a telangiectatic osteosarcoma. Low-power microscopy of the biopsy demonstrated a hemorrhagic cystic mass with solid tumor components at the cyst wall (Fig. 4a). High-power examination revealed malignant pleomorphic cells with enlarged hyperchromatic nuclei, prominent nucleoli, and filigree neoplastic osteoid adjacent to pools of hemorrhage, diagnostic of telangiectatic osteosarcoma (Fig. 4b). The tumor was composed of cytologically malignant polyhedral cells with enlarged hyperchromatic nuclei and eosinophilic cytoplasm. Multiple mitotic figures were observed, along with areas of necrosis and hemorrhagic cystic change. Immunohistochemistry showed that the tumor cells exhibited positivity for SATB-2 and the expression of INI-1 was retained. The p53 stain exhibited a mutant pattern with loss of expression. The patient was started on neoadjuvant chemotherapy consisting of adriamycin, ifosfamide, and methotrexate. After completion of the neoadjuvant regimen, she underwent wide resection of the proximal humerus through an extended deltopectoral approach. The defect was reconstructed with an allograft-prosthetic composite hemiarthroplasty with a Pyrocarbon humeral head (Fig. 5). She was discharged home the following day with no complications. The resected specimen measured 6.5 cm (Fig. 3b), and on microscopic review, 100% necrosis was observed with widely negative margins (Fig. 3c, Fig. 3d, e). At her 18-month follow-up, the wound was well healed. Although active shoulder range of motion remained limited, she was able to passively abduct and forward flex to 90° and could place her hand over her mouth and on top of her head. Her Musculoskeletal Tumor Society functional score was 25/30. There was no clinical evidence of disease recurrence, and the reconstruction remained stable.
