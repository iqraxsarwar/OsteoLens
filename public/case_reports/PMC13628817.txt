Case presentation
A 66-year-old asymptomatic woman was incidentally found to have an abnormal shadow on chest imaging, with no relevant medical history or carcinogen exposure. Contrast-enhanced computed tomography (CT) revealed a well-defined, poorly enhancing, calcified mass measuring 37 × 31 × 21 mm in the anterior mediastinum, adjacent to the left costal arch near the epigastric region (Fig. 1a). Bone-window CT, together with coronal and sagittal reformats, did not clearly demonstrate continuity between the mass and the adjacent costal cartilage (Fig. 1b-d). Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) showed T1-weighted hyperintensity with signal loss on fat-suppressed imaging, consistent with fat (Fig. 2a, b); T2-weighted imaging showed low signal within the calcified portion and high signal elsewhere, with no invasion of surrounding structures (Fig. 2c). Positron emission tomography-computed tomography (PET-CT) showed low fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG) uptake and no distant metastasis (Fig. 2d). Tumor markers were within normal limits: carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) 2.8 ng/mL (reference range < 6.0 ng/mL), alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) 4.3 ng/mL (reference range < 10.0 ng/mL), and squamous cell carcinoma antigen (SCC) 0.5 ng/mL (reference range < 1.5 ng/mL). A preoperative diagnosis of mature teratoma was made, and resection was planned.

The patient underwent RATS under general anesthesia with one-lung ventilation in the left semi-lateral decubitus position, using three 1-cm ports at the fifth intercostal space (midaxillary line), third intercostal space (anterior axillary line), and sixth intercostal space (anterior to the anterior axillary line) (Fig. 3a), with carbon dioxide insufflation at 5 mmHg. The tumor was not visible from the right thoracic cavity but was identified after incising the mediastinal pleura; dissection of its cranial aspect entered the left pleural cavity, where part of it was exposed, without adhesions. The tumor occupied the narrow space between the heart and sternum (Fig. 3b), contacting the left fifth through seventh costal cartilages with direct continuity to the sixth (Fig. 3c). Because the boundary between the tumor and normal cartilage could not be identified, the sixth costal cartilage was divided with bipolar energy applied through the robotic Maryland forceps, following the plane in which division was possible, and the specimen was removed in one piece; the sixth intercostal incision was extended to 3 cm for extraction. Operative time was 90 min, with 2 mL blood loss.

The postoperative course was uneventful, and the patient was discharged on postoperative day 4. Gross examination showed a well-circumscribed, firm mass with a glistening cartilaginous surface and focal calcification (Fig. 4a). Hematoxylin and eosin staining showed chondrocyte-like tumor cells with mild nuclear atypia, consistent with grade 1 chondrosarcoma of the costal cartilage (Fig. 4b). No uninvolved costal cartilage was identified in the specimen and the tumor-cartilage boundary was indistinct; although no definite tumor was seen at the inked margin, sectioning and crush artifact precluded reliable assessment, so residual tumor could not be excluded (Rx) and the resection was at most marginal. CT and MRI one month after surgery showed no residual disease. Wide resection would ordinarily have been indicated, but surveillance was proposed given its morbidity and grade 1 histology; the patient agreed. No recurrence has been observed at 18 months.
