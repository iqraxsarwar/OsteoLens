Case presentation
A 26-year-old woman with no significant medical history was referred with a two-year history of progressively worsening left knee pain, distal-thigh swelling, and functional limitation. She denied trauma, fever, constitutional symptoms, or recent weight loss. The pain increasingly limited ambulation and activities of daily living. Examination revealed a firm, well-circumscribed swelling over the anteromedial distal femur with localized tenderness. Knee motion was limited from 0 degrees of extension to 80 degrees of flexion because of pain. The overlying skin was intact, and distal neurovascular findings were normal.

Anteroposterior and lateral radiographs demonstrated an expansile osteolytic lesion of the distal femoral metaphysis with cortical thinning and disruption, raising concern for an aggressive primary bone tumor (Figure 1). Computed tomography showed a well-defined expansile lytic lesion extending from the distal metaphysis toward the epiphysis, with marked cortical thinning, focal posterior cortical destruction, and extension to the subchondral bone; no matrix mineralization or pathological fracture was identified (Figure 2). Chest radiography and thoracoabdominal computed tomography showed no pulmonary or visceral metastases.

Open biopsy demonstrated numerous osteoclast-like multinucleated giant cells uniformly distributed within a mononuclear stromal-cell background, confirming GCTB. Based on the extensive cortical destruction and extraosseous extension, the lesion was classified as Campanacci grade III. Histopathological examination of the resection specimen subsequently confirmed the diagnosis and negative surgical margins (Figures 3, 4).

After multidisciplinary evaluation, limb-salvage surgery was selected because extensive cortical destruction, involvement of both femoral condyles, and articular compromise made intralesional treatment unsuitable. Through a medial approach, the distal femur was resected en bloc with oncological margins, including both condyles, the metaphyseal region, adjacent soft tissue, and the medial meniscus. The femoral medullary canal was sequentially prepared, and reconstruction was performed using a cemented modular distal femoral megaprosthesis incorporating a rotating-hinge knee articulation (Figure 5). Prosthetic alignment, limb length, and joint stability were verified before definitive implantation.

The postoperative course was uneventful. During the first 48 hours, monitoring focused on hemodynamic stability, wound surveillance, and pain control; thromboprophylaxis was administered. Rehabilitation began on postoperative day 3 with supervised active and passive knee range-of-motion exercises, quadriceps strengthening, and progressive weight-bearing ambulation using a four-point walker, advanced according to pain tolerance and functional recovery. At three weeks, the incision had healed completely and the sutures were removed. Radiographs showed appropriate alignment and stable fixation of the megaprosthesis without fracture, loosening, or dislocation (Figure 6), and rehabilitation was advanced according to institutional protocol.

At the 12-month follow-up, the patient achieved a Musculoskeletal Tumor Society (MSTS) score of 26/30 (87%), demonstrating substantial pain relief and satisfactory functional recovery, with radiographs confirming stable prosthetic fixation and clinical and imaging surveillance showing no evidence of local recurrence or distant metastasis.
